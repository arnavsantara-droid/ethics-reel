# CFA Reel — 2027 FSA + Equity syllabus map

Basis: CFA Institute 2027 Level I Topic Outlines. The 2027 topic outline controls scope; current CFA Institute refresher readings are used only as supporting technical guidance where the 2027 LOS remains consistent. The 2027 curriculum-change notice is additionally used for the revised Equity emphasis on practical forecasting, scenario analysis, research reports, and factor-model context.

## Financial Statement Analysis (FSA)

### 1. Introduction to Financial Statement Analysis
**Examinable areas:** analysis framework and roles; regulatory filings; notes and supplementary schedules; management commentary; audit reports; implications of alternative reporting systems; monitoring reporting-standard changes; information sources beyond annual/interim reports.

**High-value application:** choosing relevant evidence for a decision, distinguishing audit assurance from business sustainability, reconciling comparability differences, and deciding when supplementary information changes an initial ratio-based conclusion.

### 2. Analyzing Income Statements
**Examinable areas:** revenue recognition; expense recognition; capitalization versus expensing; non-recurring/discontinued/unusual items; changes in accounting policies; basic and diluted EPS including antidilutive securities; common-size income statements and income-statement ratios.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- Basic EPS = (net income − preferred dividends) / weighted-average common shares.
- Diluted EPS: adjust numerator and denominator for dilutive instruments; exclude antidilutive instruments.
- Common-size income statement item = item / revenue.

**Common traps:** cash collection ≠ revenue recognition; capitalization changes timing rather than lifetime economics; using ending shares instead of weighted-average shares; failing to apply stock splits retroactively; including antidilutive instruments; treating management's “non-recurring” label as dispositive.

### 3. Analyzing Balance Sheets
**Examinable areas:** intangible assets; goodwill; financial instruments; non-current liabilities; common-size balance sheets and related ratios.

**High-value application:** distinguishing economic similarity from accounting presentation, evaluating recognition/measurement effects, and using notes to assess comparability.

### 4. Analyzing Statements of Cash Flows I
**Examinable areas:** links among cash flow, income statement, and balance sheet; direct and indirect preparation; deriving cash flows from statements; indirect-to-direct conversion; IFRS versus US GAAP classifications.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- Indirect CFO starts from net income and reverses non-cash items plus working-capital changes.
- Cash paid to suppliers and cash collected from customers can be reconstructed from income-statement and balance-sheet changes.

**Common traps:** confusing classification with total cash economics; wrong sign on working-capital changes; treating non-cash expenses as cash outflows; assuming IFRS and US GAAP classifications are identical.

### 5. Analyzing Statements of Cash Flows II
**Examinable areas:** reported and common-size cash-flow analysis; FCFF; FCFE; performance and coverage cash-flow ratios.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- FCFF = CFO + interest expense × (1 − tax rate) − fixed-capital investment, when using CFO constructed after interest under the relevant convention.
- FCFE = CFO − fixed-capital investment + net borrowing.
- Common-size cash flow can be scaled to revenue or another appropriate base for comparison.

**Common traps:** mixing firm and equity cash flows; forgetting after-tax interest when bridging from CFO to FCFF; treating higher CFO caused only by classification as better economics.

### 6. Analysis of Inventories
**Examinable areas:** lower of cost and net realizable value; inflation/deflation effects across inventory methods; financial-statement and ratio implications; inventory disclosures and analyst concerns.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- Inventory turnover = COGS / average inventory.
- Days inventory ≈ days in period / inventory turnover.

**Common traps:** reversing inflation effects on COGS/inventory/profit; ignoring write-down effects on future ratios; comparing companies without normalizing material inventory-method differences.

### 7. Analysis of Long-Term Assets
**Examinable areas:** purchased versus internally developed versus business-combination intangibles; impairment; derecognition; PP&E/intangible disclosures.

**High-value application:** tracing capitalization/impairment choices through assets, profit, margins, turnover, and return ratios; separating accounting loss from current cash flow.

### 8. Topics in Long-Term Liabilities and Equity
**Examinable areas:** lease reporting for lessors and lessees; defined contribution versus defined benefit plans; stock-based compensation; presentation and disclosures for long-term liabilities and share-based compensation.

**Common traps:** ignoring the balance-sheet and cash-flow consequences of financing commitments; confusing compensation expense with cash settlement; assuming all pension plans place the same risk on the issuer.

### 9. Analysis of Income Taxes
**Examinable areas:** accounting profit versus taxable income; taxes payable versus tax expense; temporary versus permanent differences; DTA/DTL creation and analytical treatment; effective/statutory/cash tax rates; tax-note and rate-reconciliation analysis.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- Effective tax rate = income tax expense / accounting profit before tax.
- Temporary differences can create DTA/DTL; permanent differences do not reverse into deferred tax balances.

**Common traps:** treating all book-tax differences as temporary; assuming DTA always has full economic value; confusing tax expense with taxes paid.

### 10. Financial Reporting Quality
**Examinable areas:** reporting quality versus quality of reported results; quality spectrum; conservative versus aggressive accounting; management incentives and enabling conditions; disciplinary mechanisms and limitations; non-GAAP presentation choices; accounting choices/estimates used to manage earnings/cash flow/balance sheet; manipulation warning signs.

**High-value application:** detecting whether a favorable result is economically strong or merely aggressively presented, and distinguishing biased presentation from outright accounting noncompliance.

### 11. Financial Analysis Techniques
**Examinable areas:** analytical tools and limitations; activity, liquidity, solvency, profitability ratios; relationships among ratios; company evaluation; DuPont ROE; industry-specific ratios; using ratios and other techniques to forecast earnings.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities.
- Quick ratio = quick assets / current liabilities.
- Receivables turnover = revenue / average receivables; days receivables ≈ days / turnover.
- Inventory turnover = COGS / average inventory; days inventory ≈ days / turnover.
- Asset turnover = revenue / average total assets.
- Net profit margin = net income / revenue.
- Financial leverage = average total assets / average equity.
- Three-part DuPont ROE = net profit margin × asset turnover × financial leverage.

**Common traps:** interpreting a single ratio without drivers; using mismatched average/ending balances; concluding that leverage-driven ROE improvement is operating improvement; comparing ratios across materially different accounting policies.

### 12. Introduction to Financial Statement Modeling
**Examinable areas:** sales-based pro forma modeling; behavioral biases in forecasts and remedies; translating Porter competitive position into prices/costs; forecasting sales/costs under inflation/deflation; choosing explicit forecast horizon and post-short-term projection assumptions.

**High-value application:** linked forecasts in which revenue, margins, reinvestment, working capital, financing, and competitive economics remain internally consistent.

## Equity Investments

### 1. Equity Instrument Features
**Examinable areas:** basic features/types of equity instruments; public versus private equity securities.

**Common traps:** confusing residual common claims with preferred priority; assuming equity classes have identical rights; treating accounting book value as market value.

### 2. Equity Jurisdictions, Classes, and the Voting Process
**Examinable areas:** economic/voting rights across jurisdictions and classes; voting process; roles of management, boards, proxy advisers, asset managers, and asset owners.

**High-value application:** separating economic ownership from control and evaluating who actually exercises or influences voting rights.

### 3. Equity Issuance and Trading
**Examinable areas:** primary versus secondary public markets; exchange, off-exchange, and OTC trading; liquidity measures; float; average daily volume; types of equity indexes.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- Float = issued/outstanding shares available for public trading after excluding restricted/strategic holdings as appropriate to the definition used.
- Average daily volume = total volume over measurement period / trading days.

**Common traps:** equating high share count with high float; treating ADV as sufficient by itself to establish low transaction cost; confusing primary financing with secondary investor-to-investor trading.

### 4. Sources of Equity Returns
**Examinable areas:** dividends, repurchases, stock splits, reverse splits; dividend chronology; price return and total return.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- Price return = (P1 − P0) / P0.
- Total return = (P1 − P0 + cash distributions) / P0, with appropriate split adjustment.

**Common traps:** treating splits as value creation; ignoring dividends in total return; confusing ex-dividend chronology with payment date.

### 5. Introduction to Equity Valuation
**Examinable areas:** price versus value; book equity, market capitalization and enterprise value as valuation indicators; major valuation-model categories and trade-offs.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- Market capitalization = share price × shares outstanding.
- Enterprise value conceptually bridges equity value to the value of operating assets available to all capital providers by adjusting for debt-like claims and non-operating cash/items as appropriate.

**Common traps:** comparing an enterprise-value numerator with an equity-only denominator; equating market price with intrinsic value; assuming book value is a universal floor.

### 6. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) and Growth Models
**Examinable areas:** DDM, FCFE, FCFF, and residual-income model inputs; PV valuation process; constant and multistage growth; shortcomings of growth assumptions; non-callable/non-convertible preferred value; contingency features.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- Gordon growth: V0 = D1 / (r − g), requiring r > g for a stable perpetuity.
- Multistage value = PV of explicit-stage cash flows + PV of terminal value.
- Perpetual non-callable preferred stock: V = dividend / required return.
- FCFF valuation discounts cash flow to all capital providers at an appropriate firm-level discount rate, then bridges to equity value; FCFE is discounted at required return on equity.
- Residual-income value starts from book value and adds PV of expected residual income.

**Common traps:** using D0 instead of D1; terminal value at the wrong date; g ≥ r in a stable perpetuity; using WACC for FCFE or cost of equity for FCFF; assuming a high near-term growth rate can continue indefinitely.

### 7. Relative Value Equity Valuation Approaches
**Examinable areas:** comparables and forecasted-fundamentals methods; price and enterprise-value multiples; appropriate uses; peer-group selection by industry or return drivers; trailing/current/forward values.

**Numerical/formula core:** P/E, P/B, P/S and appropriate EV multiples such as EV/EBITDA; justified multiples can be related to forecast fundamentals when the underlying model assumptions are coherent.

**Common traps:** using P/E with negative or transitory earnings; peer selection based only on broad industry labels; mixing trailing numerator with forward denominator; comparing EV and equity multiples inconsistently; ignoring accounting-policy differences.

### 8. Financial Statement Forecasting in Equity Valuation
**Examinable areas:** rationale/construction/use of forecast models; model design based on subject-company characteristics; estimate/interpret value from forecast-model outputs.

**2027 emphasis:** disaggregated forecasts separately model revenue, margins, investment, and financing; probability-weighted scenario analysis is introduced as standard valuation practice.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- Probability-weighted value = Σ(probability_i × scenario value_i).

**Common traps:** applying one growth rate to every line; forecasting growth without reinvestment or financing; internally inconsistent scenarios; probabilities chosen to force a preferred answer rather than reflect evidence.

### 9. Industry and Competitive Analysis
**Examinable areas:** analysis process; industry classification/grouping; size, growth, profitability, market-share trends; Porter Five Forces; PESTLE.

**2027 emphasis:** Porter/PESTLE should feed the valuation narrative rather than sit as isolated frameworks.

**Common traps:** misclassifying an external PESTLE change as a Porter force; extrapolating current margins without testing entry/rivalry/buyer/supplier/substitute pressures.

### 10. Company Analysis: Past, Present, and Future
**Examinable areas:** competitive strategy/position; revenue drivers; operating profitability; pricing power; working capital; capital investment; capital structure.

**High-value application:** translating operational drivers into revenue, margins, reinvestment, free cash flow, financing needs, and downside resilience.

### 11. Equity Analyst Research Reports
**Examinable areas:** elements of a detailed report; sell-side versus buy-side reports and conclusions; why identical modeling frameworks can produce different values due to revenue, profitability, investment, and financing assumptions.

**2027 emphasis:** research-report analysis includes initiating coverage and activist-short context in the curriculum update.

**Common traps:** treating target price as sufficient without thesis/assumptions/risks; assuming same model = same value; accepting or rejecting activist-short work solely because of the author's position.

### 12. CAPM, Market Model, and Other Factor-Based Equity Models
**Examinable areas:** use of CAPM and market model to estimate required return on equity; APT; importance of multifactor models.

**Numerical/formula core:**
- CAPM required return: r = Rf + beta × (E[Rm] − Rf).

**Common traps:** multiplying beta by total market return instead of market risk premium; pricing idiosyncratic volatility as if it were systematic beta risk; treating historical beta as automatically representative after a major business change; confusing the empirical market-model intercept with an extra CAPM premium; assuming similar market beta means identical exposure to every systematic factor.

## Question-design emphasis used in this build

The production bank deliberately overweights areas that naturally support difficult but fair Level I application: EPS and cash-flow reconstruction; classification and multi-statement effects; reporting-quality diagnostics; ratios/DuPont; DCF timing and claim-consistent discounting; peer/multiple consistency; 2027 disaggregated forecasting and scenario weighting; Porter/PESTLE-to-valuation links; company-driver analysis; research-report assumptions; CAPM/beta/multifactor interpretation.

No question is intended to require Level II/III material or confidential/recalled exam content.
